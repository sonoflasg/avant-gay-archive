An essay by [[Brian Droitcour]] in [[Right Click Save]], published on 4 May 2026, the day the group show [[New Bad Image]], curated by [[sssluke]], went live on Verse. Its subtitle: "Brian Droitcour on artists who understand what the algorithm wants and push until it breaks."

### What it argues

- "Optimization reduces friction; the new bad image adds friction back through excess. It's artmaxxing."
- On [[MiFella]]: "This is what Mifella did with Mifella (2022), the eponymous NFT collection that hollowed the face of MiladyMaker into a semi-human skull, then filled it with digital effects and distortions." The show includes Mifella's *Deep Fried Milady* (2026). See also [[Milady]].
- Mifella and [[luhhfella]] (written "Luhfella" there) are, he says, "restoring some kind of feeling to the digital image — while also laughing at the impossibility of doing so."
- On trading cards: [[terrorism]] (Terrorism) layers cards with devotional and Renaissance imagery. [[Evil Biscuit]], in his new series (the illustration is a still from *Blessing/Curse*, 2026; see [[Blessing-Curse; Preliminary Dimensional Drifella Composer & Breeder]]), "constructs collages that use reproductions of paintings as backdrops for animated sprites."
- [[Parker Ito]] (*[[Fashion Terrorist]]*, 2026) and [[Tojiba Brand Manager]] (*Untitled Video Booklet*, 2026) "pile natively digital and photographed analog textures into high-density collages."
- On anonymity: "I can't say what generation artists like Mifella, Luhfella, and Terrorism belong to... With anonymous artists, any kind of biographical interpretation is fraught, or impossible."

Other artists discussed include Ann Hirsch and Maya Man, and Petra Cortright.
