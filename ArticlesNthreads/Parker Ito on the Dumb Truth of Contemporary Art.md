An interview with [[Parker Ito]] by Alex Estorick, editor-in-chief of [[Right Click Save]], published on 12 Oct 2026. Its subtitle: "The artist discusses postinternet, Zombie Formalism, and the Avant NFT scene, coining a new term in the process." It coincided with Ito's show *Happiness is a Butterfly, Full Metal Bunny* at Rose Easton, London (see [[Happiness Is a Butterfly]] and [[Full Metal Bunny]]).

### What it says about the scene

- **The scene.** The introduction says Ito "has found kinship with the 'Avant Gay NFT' scene, a loosely organized community centering on the Solana blockchain whose derivative-driven, trait-maxed imagery Ito recognized as a continuation of collage strategies he has been developing since art school."
- **Estorick's framing.** "I regard the Avant NFT scene as an iteration of that movement [crypto art] that innovated aesthetically in a way the original didn't. Artists such as yourself as well as Evil Biscuit and Mifella have used 'trait maxing' to layer up digital objects to the point of saturation." See [[Evil Biscuit]], [[MiFella]] and [[trait-maxxing]].
- **Ito on [[Drilady]].** "Drilady is probably my favorite NFT collection that I've made, Although I regard Horses?7 as my most important artwork." It "riffed on Drifella by Evil Biscuit; it's derivatives of derivatives of derivatives." He also says: "A lot of the Solana people started doing crazy shit with Hashlips, pushing it into this kind of Dadaesque territory, which is called 'trait maxing'. I have basically no knowledge of NFTs outside of the Avant Gay scene."
- **[[Horses?7]].** Ito says it is "a follow-up to a collection called Horses?2 (2025), which is derivative of a collection called Horses? (2023) by Tojiba Brand Manager." See [[Horses?2]], [[Horses?]] and [[Tojiba Brand Manager]]. "With Horses?7 it is: can a horse be a slot machine?" He planned a Horses?7-themed painting for Art Basel Miami Beach.
- **"Derivative."** "The term 'derivative' means something really different in the Solana scene from what it means to trad art people, for whom it is a pejorative." The interview also discusses [[Profile Pictures]], made with [[Xanthrax]].
- **"Dumb Art."** The term is coined in this conversation. The article defines it in part as "an embrace of image excess where different histories and media are jammed together," which involves "using tools for the wrong purposes."
