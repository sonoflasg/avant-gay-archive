An article and interview by Dennis Koch in *Bitcoin Magazine* (Culture section), published on 18 Aug 2026: "The Pack Is the Painting: Evil Biscuit and the Schizocollage Movement Come to Hong Kong." BMAG is Bitcoin Magazine's conference art gallery. The piece ran ahead of its Card Expo at Bitcoin Asia 2026 in Hong Kong.

### What it says about the scene

- **The scene.** Koch describes "a scene of mostly pseudonymous artists, formed across crypto subcultures, Twitter timelines, and private group chats." Of the style: "They called the style schizocollage or traitmaxxing, a practice of taking the trait system that generates ordinary avatar collections and pushing it past its breaking point." He adds that the participants "settled on their own name for it: Avant/Gay." See [[Schizocollage]], [[trait-maxxing]] and [[Avant Gay]].
- **Press it cites.** It cites Dean Kissick in *Spike* ([[The Vulgar Image]]), Right Click Save's "Avant NFT underground" ([[Notes from the Avant NFT Underground]]), Peter Bauman at [[Le Random]], and [[Brian Droitcour]] on trading cards.
- **[[Evil Biscuit]].** "Near the center of the scene sits Biscuit. His Drifella collections are widely cited as its defining works." [[Parker Ito]] "credits Drifella 2 with establishing the aesthetic the whole scene became known for" (see [[Drifella 2]], [[Heavy Liquid Graphic]]).
- **[[Card NFT 2]].** It "debuted with preliminary 'Framed Cards' through SOLOS Gallery at Felix Art Fair this February" (see [[Framed Cards]], [[SOLOS]]). Biscuit made "roughly eleven thousand cards, sold in packs," each "redeemable for a physical one if the collector is willing to burn the NFT."
- **Hong Kong.** Biscuit contributed to "the Bitcoin Asia 2026 conference card pack produced by the artist Rax." His cards appear alongside work by "Ariamis (formerly Terrorism)" ([[terrorism]]). See [[Bitcoin Asia Exclusive Card]].
- **In the interview,** Biscuit says: "I would say most of the collectors in the Solana & adjacent scene agree with the sentiment that rarity doesn't matter to them." He also describes the packs on "mons dot shop" as mints "wrapped in a sealed package that can be kept unopened forever."
