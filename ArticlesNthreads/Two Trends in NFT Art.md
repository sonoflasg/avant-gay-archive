“Two Trends in NFT Art” is a two-part essay by [[Brian Droitcour]] published on biānjiè.systems on 9 May 2026. It names blockchain formalism and [[Schizocollage]] as “the most significant trends in NFT art of the last few years.”

### On schizocollage

- He defines it as “maximalist digital collage that crams wildly different styles and references into small images.” Unlike blockchain formalism, it “can exist without NFTs. In fact, it did.” Its NFT makers “use the same software tools as makers of normal NFTs, but they misuse them and overuse them.”
- He argues that “artistic explorations of maximalist digital collage preceded the NFT market.” He sets the genre against Polyvore and twentieth-century collage, and calls it “pluralist and dispersed.”
- He describes cuckcore.de as “a hub and an archive for Gay NFTs.” He calls Gay NFTs “a splinter group of artists who appreciate the visual language of Remilia and want to explore it further without taking part in the social messiness” of Remilia. He declines to retell Remilia’s controversies himself.
- He calls [[MiFella]] “an appropriation of MiladyMaker’s neo-chibi style but with boys instead of girls.” He says [[Drifella]], by [[Evil Biscuit]], “blow[s] the format up.” He describes [[Parker Ito]]’s [[Drilady]] as “schizocollage karaoke.”
- “Schizocollage has been current in the NFT scene for a few years now.”

The essay does not claim that Droitcour coined the word.
