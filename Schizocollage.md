Schizocollage is the name used for the maximalist digital collage associated with the [[Avant Gay]] scene. Brian Droitcour defines it as images that cram “wildly different styles and references into small images.” In NFT collections it is usually produced by [[trait-maxxing]] with tools such as [[Hashlips]].

*Rewritten 14/11/2026 from public sources. It replaces a May 2026 Grok draft (flagged needs-sources) that said the term was “coined by” [[Brian Droitcour]]. No source found supports that claim; see “Origins” below.*

### Origins of the term, side by side

- **Remilia Wiki:** “a visual and conceptual aesthetic coined by Brian Droitcour in a discussion of the Avant NFT Wave and Gay NFT scene.” No source is given. The earlier Grok draft closely followed this page (inference).
- **The Automat paper (Giannis Sourdis, 9 Nov 2026):** the concept “appears to predate the movement itself. By around 2021, the term circulated within online communities on platforms such as 4chan and Twitter,” for “densely layered, chaotic, and hyperreferential images.” Within Avant/Gay, “Writers such as Brian Droitcour helped formalize the term.”
- **Droitcour’s own writing:** he uses the word as an existing term and does not claim it. In [[Two Trends in NFT Art]] (9 May 2026) he writes that schizocollage “can exist without NFTs. In fact, it did,” and that maximalist digital collage “preceded the NFT market.”

The 2021 4chan and Twitter uses the Automat paper mentions have not yet been located for this archive.

### What writers say it is

- **Droitcour, [[Seams and Synthesis]] (Le Random, 2 Feb 2026):** schizocollage “foregrounds visible seams and difference,” where generative AI “synthesizes difference into coherent wholes.” He reads [[Evil Biscuit]]’s [[Drifella]] collages as images that “insist on their own stitched-together-ness.” He places [[Supermetal Bosch]]’s Mons and [[Little Swag World]] “adjacent to (but not inside) the avant-NFT milieu,” because they use AI and are not trait-based.
- **Droitcour, Two Trends in NFT Art:** artists “use the same software tools as makers of normal NFTs, but they misuse them and overuse them”; schizocollage is “pluralist and dispersed.” He describes [[Parker Ito]]’s [[Drilady]] as “schizocollage karaoke.” Artists from [[post-internet art]] have brought their own take to it.
- **GardenParty85, [[Notes from the Avant NFT Underground]]:** collections pushed “to the limits of formal superabundance through what has been termed ‘trait maxing’ or ‘schizocollage’.” HashLips generation is a “technological spin on Surrealist automatism.” The results “ape the aesthetics of cubist collage as well as the original DADA movement” ([[Cubism]], [[Dada]]).
- **Le Random:** its timeline calls [[Drifella III]] Evil Biscuit’s “hyper-‘schizocollage’ project.”
- **The Automat paper:** elements “retain traces of their distinct origins even as they are compressed into a single image.” Schizocollage is the wider aesthetic; traitmaxxing is “a generative framework.”

### Relation to trait-maxxing

The sources treat the two as overlapping, not identical. Schizocollage names the look, and can be made by hand or without NFTs. [[trait-maxxing]] names the generative method of overloading a PFP compiler. Examples named across the sources include [[Drifella]], [[Drifella 2]] and [[Drifella III]] (Evil Biscuit) and [[Drilady]] (Parker Ito). Droitcour treats Bosch’s Mons and Little Swag World as related but outside trait logic; earlier notes in this vault grouped them with schizocollage (see also [[Super Metal Mons!]]).
