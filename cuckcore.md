[[Cuckcore]] is an earlier, more antagonistic name and milieu inside the history that later writing often folds into [[Avant Gay]]. This note was empty in the vault. It is compiled by SonOfLasG from public sources already in the archive and from the [[Automat Avant-Gay paper]]. It is not a complete history of cuckcore.de, and it does not use private chats.

### In this archive already

[[SonOfLasG]]’s primer [[All Roads Lead to MiFella]] (15 Sep 2025, https://x.com/Sonoflasg/status/1967509090550394906) describes trawling “[[Cuckcore]] dot de,” abandoned Discords, and semi-deleted threads in order to follow [[MiFella]] as the fracture from [[Milady]]. ### Public outline

The Automat paper keeps Cuckcore distinct from “Avant NFT.” It describes a phase rooted in parody, implication, derivation, and market play, often hostile to the more self-serious claims around Avant NFT. [[MiFella]] identifies cuckcore.de, in that paper, as the best surviving — if still incomplete — archive of the early MiFella and Cuckcore milieu. [[Brian Droitcour]], in a written interview used by the paper (September 2026), is quoted there as calling cuckcore.de a “living archive” of Gay NFT, valuable because it does not stand fully outside the culture it records.

### Accounts that differ

**On what the name covers.** An early participant, speaking anonymously to Sourdis, emphasized that Cuckcore was not interchangeable with Avant NFT, and that opposition to Avant NFT’s self-seriousness was partly constitutive of it. [[Tojiba Brand Manager]], in a July 2026 written interview used by the same paper, defines Cuckcore more narrowly as work and activity rooted in the MiFella “cold pot,” and warns that the later umbrella name [[Avant Gay]] can flatten that history: for him it was an “interscene phenomenon” whose images mattered because of group-chat connections and derivative activity, not only because of a messy-collage look.

**On tone.** The paper states that Cuckcore’s antagonism was not always symbolic, that rivalries and factional disputes could enter the work itself, and that “removing this hostility from the history would present Cuckcore as more coherent and culturally generous than it often felt to those involved.” That is recorded as the paper’s caution, not as a roster of accusations.

**On later reception.** Droitcour, in the same written interview as cited by the paper, singles out [[Cuckfella]], [[Chucks]], and [[Chair NFT]] as works that lean hard into the language of the cucking fetish, which he suggests may help explain why they are discussed less often later. The paper marks this as interpretive rather than demonstrable.
